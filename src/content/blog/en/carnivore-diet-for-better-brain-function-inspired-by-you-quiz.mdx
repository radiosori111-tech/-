## 📌 Key Takeaways

> **The short answer:** The early side effects of a carnivore or keto diet, the so-called **keto flu**, usually appeared in the first week and faded **within a month**. But gut bacteria changed within days, and benefits like healing a leaky gut or easing autoimmune disease haven't been confirmed in experiments. The safe approach is to drink plenty of water, cut carbs gradually, and stop the diet if symptoms drag on.

- 🤒 **Keto flu usually ended within a month.** An analysis of people's own accounts of keto flu found that headache, fatigue, nausea, dizziness and **brain fog** appeared mostly in the first week and usually faded within four weeks (**Frontiers in Nutrition**, 2020).
- 🧠 **The brain can run on ketone bodies.** In people who fasted for a long time, **ketone bodies** supplied more than half of the brain's fuel (**Journal of Clinical Investigation**, 1967).
- 🦠 **Gut bacteria changed within days.** Eating only meat, eggs and cheese increased bile-tolerant bacteria and decreased bacteria that break down fiber (**Nature**, 2014).
- 🫘 **Lectins disappear with cooking.** Lectins in raw kidney beans caused food poisoning, but thorough boiling removed their toxicity (**BMJ**, 1980).

### 💬 "I started carnivore, so why is my head so foggy?"

This post began as a guide for people starting the carnivore diet, with methods, menus and early side effects. In an earlier post, [What Is the Carnivore Diet? 2,000 Satisfied People, and Rising LDL](/en/blog/what-is-the-carnivore-diet-people-say-changed-their-brains/), I summarized survey results and risks from people who've tried it. This post answers the next question: "What happens to your body when you start, and how long does the discomfort last?"

The original post had many strong lines like "plant toxins destroy the gut barrier," "it dramatically relieves autoimmune disease" and "false hunger disappears completely." This time I looked up the original papers and separated what held up from what needed correcting.

## Deep Dive: What happens to the body and brain in carnivore week one?

### 1. What is keto flu, and how long does it last?

A team at the University of Tasmania in Australia gathered and analyzed accounts of "keto flu" that people posted themselves on online forums (Bostock et al., **Frontiers in Nutrition**, 2020).

- The most common symptoms were **flu-like aches**, **headache**, **fatigue**, **nausea**, **dizziness** and a foggy head, or **brain fog**.
- Symptoms mostly appeared in the **first week** of the diet and usually faded **within four weeks**.

The early side effects in the original post (brain fog and headache, lethargy and dizziness, digestive discomfort) match this well. But since the study collected posts people wrote themselves, it can't tell how many people get keto flu.

### 2. When carbs are cut off, what runs the brain?

A Harvard team compared blood entering and leaving the brain in people who fasted for five to six weeks as an obesity treatment (Owen et al., **Journal of Clinical Investigation**, 1967).

- After prolonged fasting, **ketone bodies** (beta-hydroxybutyrate and acetoacetate) supplied more than half of the brain's fuel.
- This study changed the belief at the time that the brain uses only glucose.

The original post's claim that "ketone bodies are used as brain fuel" is correct. But the claim that "the mind becomes very clear and focus, memory and emotional control are **maximized**" hasn't been confirmed in experiments with healthy people. The link between ketones and brain fog continues in [Brain Fog and Ketone Bodies](/en/blog/brain-fog-ketones-carnivore-mental-clarity/).

### 3. Why do I feel dizzy and weak at first?

The original post's explanation is mostly right. Cutting carbs lowers **insulin**, and the kidneys excrete more electrolytes like **sodium** along with water. That can make you dizzy or weak when you stand up.

But following the original post's advice that "you must keep taking 3–5 g or more of salt in water every day" as is could be risky. In a meta-analysis of 34 trials, cutting salt lowered blood pressure, with a bigger effect in people with high blood pressure (He et al., **BMJ**, 2013). If you have high blood pressure or heart or kidney disease, be sure to talk with a doctor before adding extra salt. Even healthy people should start with normal seasoning of food, and drinking enough water comes first.

### 4. What happens in the gut when you eat only meat?

A Harvard team had 10 people eat only meat, eggs and cheese for five days and analyzed their gut bacteria (David et al., **Nature**, 2014).

- Gut bacteria composition changed **within days** of starting the meat-based diet.
- Bile-tolerant bacteria (such as ***Bilophila wadsworthia***) increased, while bacteria that break down fiber decreased.

The original post said "gut flora degenerates." Change does happen, but there's no evidence it's a good change. *Bilophila wadsworthia* is a bacterium linked to colitis in mouse experiments. Also, the original claim that "removing plant fiber regenerates the gut barrier and heals leaky gut" has never been confirmed in human experiments. In fact, a large analysis found that eating more fiber was linked to lower risks of heart disease, diabetes and colorectal cancer. This is covered in detail in [What Is the Carnivore Diet](/en/blog/what-is-the-carnivore-diet-people-say-changed-their-brains/).

### 5. Are lectins and oxalates really toxins?

The original post called **lectins** and **oxalates** in spinach, beans and nuts "gut-barrier-destroying substances." The research says something a bit different.

- **Lectins**: In the UK, cases of food poisoning with vomiting and diarrhea were reported after people ate raw or undercooked kidney beans. But when beans were soaked and then **boiled thoroughly**, the lectins' toxicity disappeared (Noah et al., **BMJ**, 1980). Cooked beans rarely cause this problem.
- **Oxalates**: They're abundant in vegetables like spinach, and people prone to kidney stones are advised to limit them. But there's no basis for calling them toxins for everyone.

So the claim that "plant toxins must be eliminated entirely" goes too far. Someone who feels discomfort with a particular food adjusting that food is a different matter from cutting out all plant foods.

### 6. Does appetite really go down?

In a meta-analysis of ketogenic diet studies, people in ketosis felt less hungry than before the diet (Gibson et al., **Obesity Reviews**, 2015). The original post's claim that "appetite decreases" matches this. But the effect was a **slight** reduction, and there was no evidence that "false hunger and binge urges disappear **completely**."

**Step 1. Don't quit all at once; cut down over 2–4 weeks**
The original post said to "first clear carbs and vegetables out of the fridge," but the faster you cut, the less time your body has to adapt. Start with sugary drinks and refined carbs, keep cooked vegetables, and change gradually.

**Step 2. Plenty of water; salt only after talking with a doctor**
Early on, a lot of water and sodium leave through urine, so drink water often. Decide on extra salt only after confirming you don't have high blood pressure or heart or kidney disease, and ideally with a doctor's input.

**Step 3. Check your body at the four-week mark**
Keto flu usually faded within four weeks. If it lasts longer, or dizziness or palpitations are severe, stop the diet. If you continue, it's a good idea to check your **LDL cholesterol** with a blood test after a few months.

## Conclusion

If your head feels foggy and you're drained after starting carnivore, that's usually keto flu while your body switches fuels, and it often passed within a month. It's also true that the brain can use ketone bodies for fuel. But benefits like healing leaky gut, easing autoimmune disease and maximizing focus haven't been confirmed, and adding salt indiscriminately can be risky. Cut down gradually, drink plenty of water, and check your body at the four-week mark.

### 🙋 Frequently Asked Questions

#### Does getting keto flu mean the diet doesn't suit me?

Not necessarily. When carbs are cut suddenly, many people had similar symptoms in the first week, and they usually faded within four weeks. But if they continue past four weeks, or you have chest pain, severe palpitations or fainting, stop the diet and go to a hospital right away.

#### Is carnivore better for the brain?

It's true that the brain can use ketone bodies for fuel, but experimental evidence that it improves brain function in healthy people is lacking. If your goal is a brain-friendly diet, cutting sugary drinks and refined carbs while adding fish, vegetables and nuts has more evidence behind it.
