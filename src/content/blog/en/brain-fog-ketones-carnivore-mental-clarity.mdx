## 📌 Key Takeaways

> **The short answer:** Getting sleepy after lunch involved not just carbohydrates but the **body's daily rhythm**. Even without lunch, drowsiness came around 2–4 p.m., and sugary food actually increased fatigue within an hour. Eating a lighter lunch, then taking a short walk or a **10-minute nap**, is the approach that fits the research.

- 🕑 **Even skipping lunch, afternoons brought drowsiness.** A review of daily-rhythm research found that early-afternoon drowsiness and lower focus appeared even on days without lunch, and got worse with big meals (**Clinics in Sports Medicine**, 2005).
- 🍬 **Sugary food didn't give energy.** In a meta-analysis of 31 studies, people who ate sugar were more tired and less alert within an hour (**Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews**, 2019).
- 😴 **A 10-minute nap worked right away.** People who took a 10-minute afternoon nap were less sleepy and more alert as soon as they woke, while a 30-minute nap first brought grogginess (**Sleep**, 2006).
- 🥩 **One study found cutting carbs slightly improved memory.** When 23 older adults with mild cognitive impairment sharply cut carbs for six weeks, word memory scores rose. But it was a small study (**Neurobiology of Aging**, 2012).

### 💬 "Should I skip lunch on important days to keep a clear head?"

The saying that you need to eat to think well, I now think, is half right and half wrong. On days I needed to focus, I'd habitually skip lunch. Whenever I ate rice with meat, my energy would drain away and my eyelids would grow heavy.

Then I tried a **carnivore** diet centered on meat, butter, eggs and cheese. On the third day my head felt foggier and my body heavier, but after a week or two, the post-meal fog lessened and I felt my thinking stay clear through the afternoon.

In the first version of this post I explained this experience as "blood sugar swings from carbohydrates are the root cause of brain fog," and included the claim that salt cuts sugar cravings and examples of celebrities' diets. Looking up the original papers this time, my experience is partly explained by research, but the parts that pinned it on a single cause, and some claims, needed correcting.

## Deep Dive: Why does afternoon brain fog happen?

### 1. Do you get sleepy in the afternoon even without lunch?

Yes. Sleep researcher Timothy Monk of the University of Pittsburgh reviewed research on the "**post-lunch dip**," the drop in focus and performance in the early afternoon (Monk, **Clinics in Sports Medicine**, 2005).

- The dip was larger on days with lunch, but appeared **even on days without lunch**.
- Human sleepiness follows a daily rhythm that peaks at night, with a smaller second peak in the early afternoon.
- Short sleep or a big lunch made the dip worse.

So the earlier version's claim that "the root cause of post-meal sleepiness is blood sugar swings" is corrected. Afternoon drowsiness starts with the body's daily rhythm and lack of sleep, and the amount and type of food add on top. Feeling clear-headed when I skipped lunch on important days may also have been because overeating wasn't adding to the drowsiness.

### 2. Does sugary food really give you energy?

It did the opposite. When a University of Warwick team pooled 31 studies with 1,259 people, those who ate sugar were **more tired and less alert within an hour** than those who didn't (Mantantzis et al., **Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews**, 2019). The earlier version's term "sugar crash" points the same way as this result. How to measure blood sugar changes yourself is covered in [How to Use a Glucose Meter at Home](/en/blog/glucose-meter-at-home-brain-health-signals-from-one-drop-of-blood/).

### 3. Do ketone bodies really clear the mind?

The first version cited Frontiers in Psychology (2019) for the claim that "ketone bodies reduce inflammation in brain neurons and genuinely improve cognitive function," but the link only went to the journal's home page, so I couldn't tell which paper it was. So I replaced it with research that can be checked.

A University of Cincinnati team divided 23 older adults with mild cognitive impairment into a sharply reduced-carb diet and a high-carb diet for six weeks (Krikorian et al., **Neurobiology of Aging**, 2012).

- The reduced-carb group's **word memory** scores rose, and higher blood ketone levels went with higher memory scores.
- But there were only 23 participants, and they were older adults with mild cognitive impairment.

I haven't yet found a study testing whether carnivore clears brain fog in healthy people. The "more than 91% of 2,029 people improved memory and mental clarity" figure from the first version comes from a real Harvard team survey (Lennerz et al., **Current Developments in Nutrition**, 2021), but it was a **self-reported survey** of people already happily following carnivore, so it's hard to treat as evidence of an effect. This study is covered in detail in [What Is the Carnivore Diet?](/en/blog/what-is-the-carnivore-diet-people-say-changed-their-brains/).

### 4. Does eating salt reduce sugar cravings?

The first version introduced one researcher's claim that "when salt is low, the renin-angiotensin system overactivates into an 'internal starvation' state, and the brain seeks sugar." I couldn't find research testing this claim in people. It's true that cutting carbs makes you lose more sodium in urine, but the idea that salt water cuts sugar cravings within minutes stays as a personal experience. If you have high blood pressure or heart or kidney disease, be sure to talk with a doctor before adding extra salt.

### 5. Are celebrities' diets evidence?

The first version included examples of three people, a tennis player, a broadcaster and a psychologist, who beat brain fog by cutting gluten or eating only meat. One person's experience only means it suited that person; it isn't evidence that it works for others. Some details were also hard to verify, so I removed this part.

## Solution: 3 Steps to Reduce Afternoon Brain Fog

**Step 1. Eat a lighter lunch and skip sweets**
Rather than skipping lunch, eat a bit less and cut sugary drinks and desserts. Eating protein and vegetables first and having less rice or bread is enough. If you want to sharply cut carbs, set a time limit, and talk with a doctor first if you have a medical condition.

**Step 2. Walk for 10 minutes after eating**
After meals, walk lightly for about 10 minutes. Moving helps shake off drowsiness and can also blunt the rise in blood sugar after eating. Try the routine below as is.

**Step 3. If sleepy, close your eyes for just 10 minutes**
When a Flinders University team in Australia compared nap lengths, a **10-minute nap** reduced drowsiness and improved alertness as soon as people woke, with benefits lasting over two hours. A 30-minute nap first brought a groggy, half-awake state (Brooks & Lack, **Sleep**, 2006). Set your alarm for 10 minutes. Why you feel groggy right after waking is covered in [Why Is Your Head Foggy Right After Waking?](/en/blog/the-early-bird-the-first-30-minutes-and-sleep-inertia/).

## Conclusion

The fog that comes every afternoon after lunch isn't from weak willpower. But it wasn't just about carbohydrates either. Our bodies are built to get sleepy once in the early afternoon even without lunch, and overeating, sweets and short sleep made that drowsiness worse. My clearer afternoons on carnivore may have come from eating less and cutting sweets. Eat a lighter lunch, walk for 10 minutes after, and if you're still sleepy, close your eyes for just 10 minutes.

### 🙋 Frequently Asked Questions

#### Does coffee get rid of the post-lunch slump?

Caffeine helps reduce drowsiness, but drinking it late in the afternoon can disrupt your night's sleep and worsen the next day's afternoon slump. One approach is to close your eyes for 10–15 minutes right after coffee, so you wake as the caffeine starts working. But avoid it late in the afternoon.

#### How can I tell whether brain fog is a diet problem or a sleep problem?

First check whether you're sleeping enough. Short sleep greatly worsened the afternoon dip. If you sleep enough but still feel very foggy after every meal, start by cutting lunch portions and sweets. If brain fog continues for weeks or your memory drops noticeably, see a doctor.
