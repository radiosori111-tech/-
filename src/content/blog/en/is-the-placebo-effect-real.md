## The placebo effect really exists

If you came here wondering whether the **placebo effect** really exists, see what Dr. Monty Lyman has to say.

In my [last post](/en/blog/the-secret-of-success/), I shared the four things I'm doing right now as my secret to success. One of them was reading, and the book I briefly mentioned was <strong>Monty Lyman's</strong> <strong>*The Painful Truth*.</strong> The book says that if we cling only to the idea that pain is a direct measure of damage, we can't understand pain's strange and fickle side. It says the first step in healing body and mind is understanding that <strong>pain is a response to protect our bodies and doesn't necessarily give accurate information about tissue damage</strong> (*The Painful Truth*, p. 34).

In one experiment in the book, patients with knee pain undergo **arthroscopic debridement** (removing damaged tissue from an arthritic joint). Group A gets the real surgery, while group B only has the knee opened and closed, with no real surgery. Which group do you think showed a more positive change in pain?

Remarkably, group B showed better pain relief after surgery than group A, which had the real operation. Why do you think that is? Have you ever heard of **learned helplessness**?

Learned helplessness is the state in which, after being exposed to an aversive stimulus and repeatedly learning that you can't escape it despite trying, you no longer make any effort or attempt to escape even after the stimulus is removed. What matters in learned helplessness is that **"expectation"** and **"hope"** have disappeared. Conversely, the placebo effect means pain relief appeared thanks to a brain filled with expectation and hope.

In a way, the book can be difficult, since it covers the history and experiments of neuroscience as it relates to pain, but through it you learn about how to live.

If belief has a way of reaching the body, the words you tell yourself have their own way of reaching the brain. See [**affirmations that actually reach the brain**](/en/blog/why-affirmations-fail-even-after-1000-times-brain-science/).

If you feel right now that something hurts, your first reaction shouldn't simply be to get scared and afraid; you should take the attitude, "Ah, this is to protect my body, so I'll look after it from now on."

<strong>In life, "expectation and hope" are what move our brains and change them.</strong>

No matter where we stand right now.

I'll end with something a YouTuber said:

<strong>"I don't think success or failure is decided by where a person is right now; I think it's a person's mindset that lets them succeed or keeps them where they are."</strong>

(I've only read a third of the book so far.)
