## 📌 Key Takeaways

> **The short answer:** Holiday weight didn't all come off on its own. About **half the weight gained over the holidays was still there five months later**. But much of the weight gained in the days right after a holiday is **water**, so returning to normal meals and exercise within two weeks makes it easier to undo. Taking magnesium until you get diarrhea turned out to be **a risky method that goes past the official upper limit**.

- 🎉 **Half of holiday weight stayed.** Smart-scale records from about 3,000 people in the US, Germany and Japan showed weight rising over the holidays, and about half the weight gained around Christmas was still there five months later (**NEJM**, 2016).
- 💧 **Weight lost in a few days is mostly water.** When the body stores carbohydrates as **glycogen**, it holds onto more than three times that weight in water. So in the first few days of cutting carbs, weight dropped fast as water left (**American Journal of Clinical Nutrition**, 1992).
- 🏃 **Fasted exercise didn't burn more fat.** Over four weeks, fat loss was similar in a group exercising fasted and a group exercising after eating (**Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition**, 2014).
- 💊 **Supplemental magnesium has an upper limit.** The US National Institutes of Health set the adult upper limit for supplemental magnesium at 350 mg a day, and diarrhea is a sign of too much (**NIH Office of Dietary Supplements**).

### 💬 "How do I quickly undo the weight I gained over the holiday?"

Every time the Chuseok holiday ends, that heavy feeling comes back, and I go through it too. Pancakes, ribs, songpyeon rice cakes: sitting at the table my parents and grandparents prepared, patting my stomach and saying "that was so good," the holiday goes by.

So the morning after the holiday ends, I drink a cup of warm salt tea and walk or jog lightly for 30 minutes or more. Then for one to two weeks, I've reset my body with a **carnivore** diet centered on animal foods like leftover salt-grilled meat, skewers and eggs. The first time I tried carnivore, on the third night I had such a bad calf cramp I couldn't sleep. After that, I started taking **magnesium**.

The first version of this post wrote down exactly what I'd been doing. But one method, "taking magnesium gluconate until you get diarrhea," could be dangerous if someone else follows it. This time I looked up the original papers and official sources and separated what held up from what needed correcting.

## Deep Dive: How does holiday weight go on, and how does it come off?

### 1. Does holiday weight come off on its own?

Not all of it. A team from Tampere University of Technology in Finland and Cornell University in the US analyzed a year of weight records from about 3,000 smart-scale users in the US, Germany and Japan (Helander et al., **New England Journal of Medicine**, 2016).

- In all three countries, **weight rose noticeably during holiday periods** like Christmas and New Year, and Golden Week in Japan.
- **About half the weight gained around Christmas was still there five months after the holidays ended.**

Since the study only included people who used a scale regularly, results may differ for the general public. But it means that if you leave holiday weight thinking "it'll come off anyway," some of it may stay. The earlier version's aim of resetting right after the holiday points the right way in light of this result.

### 2. Is the weight lost in the days right after a holiday fat?

Mostly it's water. The body stores carbohydrates as **glycogen** in the liver and muscles, and holds about 3–4 g of water with every gram of glycogen (Kreitzman et al., **American Journal of Clinical Nutrition**, 1992). Eating lots of rice cakes, pancakes and songpyeon over a holiday increases glycogen and water together, making the body feel puffy and heavy.

So when you cut carbs, weight drops quickly over the first few days as glycogen and water leave. The earlier version's claim that it "quickly reverses the puffiness" is right in this sense. But this isn't fat loss, and the original's phrase "flushing out waste" has no basis. Once you eat carbs again, the water comes back. So the real goal isn't the number after a few days, but returning to your usual food intake and activity before the added **fat** settles in.

### 3. Does fasted exercise burn more fat?

The first version said "fasted cardio uses up glycogen first and doubles the effect." A US team divided 20 young women into a group doing fasted cardio and a group exercising after eating for four weeks (Schoenfeld et al., **Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition**, 2014). Both groups lost weight and body fat, but **there was no difference**.

Walking or jogging the morning after a holiday is a good habit. But **moving consistently** matters more than whether you're fasted. If fasted exercise makes you dizzy, it's fine to eat something light first.

### 4. Does carnivore make you short on magnesium?

The earlier version's explanation is mostly right. Cutting carbs lowers insulin, and the kidneys excrete more electrolytes like sodium along with water. And since **magnesium** is abundant in plant foods like nuts, beans, spinach and whole grains, eating only animal foods makes it easy to fall short. The US National Institutes of Health's recommended daily amount for adults is 310–420 mg (**NIH Office of Dietary Supplements**).

But the earlier version's explanation that "leg cramps mean magnesium deficiency" needs some correcting. In a Cochrane review of trials testing magnesium supplements for muscle cramps, magnesium was **unlikely to help** common leg cramps in older people (Garrison et al., **Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews**, 2020). Cramps can come from many causes, like low fluids, other electrolytes and exercise. Why your head can feel foggy early on carnivore is covered separately in [brain fog and ketones](/en/blog/brain-fog-ketones-carnivore-mental-clarity/).

### 5. Is it OK to take magnesium until you get diarrhea?

**No.** This is the biggest correction to the first version.

- The US National Institutes of Health set the adult **upper limit for magnesium from supplements or medications at 350 mg a day**. Taking more can cause diarrhea, nausea and stomach cramps, and diarrhea isn't a sign the body "absorbed as much as it needed" but a sign you've **already taken too much** (**NIH Office of Dietary Supplements**).
- In particular, **people with reduced kidney function** can't clear magnesium well, so large amounts can raise blood magnesium too high, causing muscle weakness or heart rhythm problems.
- The earlier version's interpretation that "people who don't get diarrhea have a lot of built-up waste" also has no basis. The amount it takes to cause diarrhea just varies with each person's gut.

An official upper limit of 350 mg a day means that taking enough to cause diarrhea already goes past that line. Deliberately causing diarrhea isn't a weight-loss method; it's something that can lead to dehydration and electrolyte imbalance.

### 6. Is magnesium oxide really poorly absorbed?

The earlier version's "magnesium oxide absorption is around 4%" is a figure that appears in actual research. When a US team compared absorption of several magnesium supplements, magnesium oxide had the lowest absorbed fraction at about 4%, while water-soluble forms like magnesium citrate, lactate and chloride were higher (Firoz & Graber, **Magnesium Research**, 2001). That's why magnesium oxide is widely used as a laxative.

But even with low absorption, each tablet contains a lot of magnesium, so the amount actually absorbed isn't tiny. And even well-absorbed forms can cause diarrhea in large amounts. That's why absorption alone can't tell you whether a form is good or bad.

### 7. Does magnesium help you sleep?

The first version called magnesium "a natural sedative that relaxes the nerves." Magnesium's role in nerves and muscles really is important, but only a few small trials in older adults with insomnia showed improvement in some sleep measures, and the evidence is still weak. My experience of sleeping well after taking magnesium stays as a personal impression.

## Solution: 3 Steps to Undo Holiday Weight

If you have kidney disease or take antibiotics, osteoporosis drugs, diuretics or acid reducers, magnesium supplements can build up in your body or interact with your medication, so talk with a doctor or pharmacist first. If leg cramps keep coming back, finding the cause with a doctor comes before simply taking more magnesium.

**Step 1. Return to normal meals within two weeks of the holiday**
From the day after the holiday, cut back on rice cakes, pancakes and sugary drinks first, and return to your usual portions. I set a limit of one to two weeks for carnivore, but sharply cutting carbs can be risky depending on your health. Returning to your normal diet with vegetables and whole grains is enough. Remember that weight lost in a few days is mostly water.

**Step 2. Walk or jog lightly for 30 minutes every day**
Fasted or not, walk or jog lightly for about 30 minutes a day. The key is raising your activity above usual before the post-holiday weight settles in. Splitting it into short bouts is fine.

**Step 3. Magnesium from food first**
Magnesium is plentiful in foods like nuts, beans and spinach. If you are thinking about supplements, check the official upper limit (350 mg a day from supplements) and your current medications with a pharmacist. Diarrhea is your body's signal that it's "too much."

## Conclusion

Holiday weight left alone stayed about halfway. So my habit of resetting right after the holiday was the right direction. But weight lost in a few days is mostly water, and fasted exercise wasn't especially more effective. Above all, don't follow the method of taking magnesium until you get diarrhea. Returning to normal meals and daily walking within two weeks, and getting magnesium from food first, is the safe way to reset after a holiday.

### 🙋 Frequently Asked Questions

#### Is it OK to do carnivore after a holiday?

Even for a short time it sharply cuts carbs, so if you have diabetes, high blood pressure or kidney disease, or take medication, you need to talk with a doctor first. It doesn't have to be carnivore; simply cutting back on rice cakes, pancakes and sugary drinks and returning to normal meals is enough. The risks and benefits of the carnivore diet are covered in [What Is the Carnivore Diet?](/en/blog/what-is-the-carnivore-diet-people-say-changed-their-brains/).

#### Is it OK to eat a lot of eggs?

Just eat your eggs cooked. **Avidin** in raw egg white binds biotin and blocks its absorption, but cooking changes avidin and greatly reduces this effect. If you've been told your cholesterol is high, though, it's a good idea to discuss your daily amount with a doctor.
