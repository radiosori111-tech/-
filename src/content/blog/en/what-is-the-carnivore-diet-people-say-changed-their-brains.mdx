## 📌 Key takeaways

> **The short answer:** The **carnivore diet** cuts out vegetables, grains, and fruit and includes only animal foods such as meat, fish, and eggs. People who follow it reported high satisfaction, but their LDL cholesterol was high, and the idea that "ketones raise BDNF in the brain" still comes from mouse studies. If it's your brain you're thinking of, **cutting refined carbs while keeping vegetables** is closer to the evidence than eating only meat.

- 🥩 **Satisfaction was high.** In a survey of 2,029 adults on a carnivore diet, most said their overall health had improved (**Current Developments in Nutrition**, 2021). But these were self-selected survey respondents.
- 🩸 **LDL cholesterol was high.** In the same survey, average LDL cholesterol was **172 mg/dL**, above typical recommended levels (2021).
- 🧠 **Ketones and BDNF are a mouse finding.** The result that the ketone β-hydroxybutyrate switches on the brain's BDNF gene came from mice (**eLife**, 2016).
- 🥦 **People who ate the most fiber lived longer.** Those with the highest fiber intake had a 15 to 30% lower risk of death, heart disease, and colorectal cancer than those with the lowest (**Lancet**, 2019).

### 💬 "I only ate meat and my head cleared?"

These days, going beyond low-carb, the **carnivore diet**, which cuts out vegetables and grains entirely and includes only meat and other animal foods, is a hot topic in health communities. Reviews keep saying it helped not just with weight but with focus and memory, and the word **BDNF** comes up as the explanation. I was skeptical at first too.

When I first wrote this post, I built it around the idea that "ketones may act as a signal that helps the brain make BDNF." Looking up the original papers this time, I needed to say more clearly what kind of experiment that result came from, and what risks the first version of this post left out.

## Deep Dive: how much is confirmed about the carnivore diet?

### 1. What exactly is the carnivore diet?

It's a diet of meat such as beef, pork, and lamb, plus fish, eggs, and a little dairy, with almost no vegetables, grains, fruit, sugar, beans, or nuts. It cuts plant foods even more drastically than the **ketogenic diet**, which limits carbs to around 20 g a day. With almost no carbs, the liver makes **ketones** from fat for the brain to use as an alternative fuel.

That ketones affect the brain is itself well established. In a randomized trial of 145 children with epilepsy that medicine couldn't control, 38% of those on a ketogenic diet for three months had their seizures cut by more than half, compared with 6% of the control group (Neal et al., **Lancet Neurology**, 2008). But that's a medically supervised therapeutic diet, which is a different matter from focus in healthy people.

### 2. How did people who actually tried it fare?

A team at Boston Children's Hospital surveyed 2,029 adults online who had followed a carnivore diet for at least six months (Lennerz et al., **Current Developments in Nutrition**, 2021). Most participants said their overall health, energy, and focus had improved, and those with diabetes reported cutting back on medication.

But two things need to be seen together.

- The respondents sought out the survey themselves, so it included almost no one who had quit because the diet didn't suit them.
- The average **LDL cholesterol** participants reported was high, at **172 mg/dL**. The researchers also said the long-term effects on heart health are unknown.

### 3. Do ketones really raise BDNF?

When I checked the original study behind what I wrote in the first version, that "research is emerging that ketones may act as a signal that helps the brain make BDNF," it turned out to be a **mouse study**. A team at Cornell University showed that the ketone **β-hydroxybutyrate** rose in the hippocampus of mice that exercised, and that this molecule flips on the BDNF gene switch (Sleiman et al., **eLife**, 2016). Interestingly, the starting point of this study wasn't diet but **exercise**.

> In the first version I wrote that "the carnivore diet is mentioned as one way to stimulate BDNF." I couldn't find a study showing that BDNF in the brain rose when people followed a carnivore diet, so I corrected this link to "a possibility from animal studies."

### 4. What do you lose when you cut out vegetables and fiber?

The biggest difference with the carnivore diet is that it has **almost no dietary fiber**.

- In an analysis of 185 observational studies and 58 clinical trials, people who ate the most fiber had a **15 to 30% lower** risk of death, heart disease, stroke, type 2 diabetes, and colorectal cancer than those who ate the least (Reynolds et al., **Lancet**, 2019).
- When people ate only animal foods for five days, their gut bacteria shifted quickly, with bile-tolerant bacteria increasing and bacteria that break down plant fiber decreasing (David et al., **Nature**, 2014).
- The World Health Organization's International Agency for Research on Cancer classified processed meat as a Group 1 carcinogen and red meat as Group 2A, and every 50 g of processed meat eaten daily raised colorectal cancer risk by 18% (Bouvard et al., **Lancet Oncology**, 2015).

## The fix: three things to know before changing your diet

The carnivore diet can be risky for people who have been told their LDL cholesterol is high, who have kidney or liver disease, who use diabetes medication or insulin, or who are pregnant or breastfeeding, so it is a diet to discuss with your medical team first.

**Step 1. Start by cutting refined carbs**
Cutting down on bread, snacks, and sugary drinks is a change you can make without going carnivore. Much of what people report as benefits, steadier blood sugar and less snacking, overlaps with this change. I covered meal order and blood sugar in [what insulin does](/en/blog/what-does-insulin-do-and-how-to-use-it-wisely/).

**Step 2. Keep vegetables and fiber**
Even if you eat mostly meat, keep low-carb plant foods such as vegetables, seaweed, and mushrooms. Protecting your fiber protects your gut bacteria and colon health. Eat fish often and cut back on processed meat.

**Step 3. Check with a blood test every three months**
If you are on a meat-heavy diet, LDL ran high in the research, so it is safest to check your **LDL cholesterol**, fasting blood sugar, and liver and kidney numbers with your medical team. Even if you feel great, the numbers may tell a different story.

## Conclusion

People on the carnivore diet were mostly satisfied, but their LDL cholesterol was high, and the price was giving up dietary fiber. The claim that "eating only meat raises BDNF and improves the brain" is still a possibility from mouse experiments. If you want to change something for your brain, start by **cutting refined carbs**, keep your vegetables, and check your body by the numbers.

### 🙋 Frequently asked questions

#### What's the difference between the ketogenic and carnivore diets?

Both cut carbs sharply, but the ketogenic diet can include plant foods such as vegetables, nuts, and olive oil, while the carnivore diet cuts them out almost entirely. That makes the carnivore diet more likely to fall short on fiber and some vitamins. I covered ketones and the feeling of a clearer head in [brain fog and ketones](/en/blog/brain-fog-ketones-carnivore-mental-clarity/).

#### Are there foods that are good for the brain?

Foods don't contain BDNF themselves, but some have been studied for helping the body make it, such as omega-3s from fish and flavonoids from berries, in both human and animal research. I sorted the evidence by strength in [Which foods are rich in BDNF?](/en/blog/which-foods-are-rich-in-bdnf/).
